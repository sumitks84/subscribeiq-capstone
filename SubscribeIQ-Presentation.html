Lifetime value is the monthly bill times the months we expect to keep them.

months left = 1 ÷ score
capped at 60
Why 1 ÷ score
In this dataset churn means leaving in the last month, so the score acts as a monthly chance. A constant chance of p gives an average life of 1 ÷ p months.
The catch
Balanced class weights inflate the scores. Use lifetime value to rank customers, not to forecast.

47 would have stayed. A false alarm costs one offer. A miss costs a customer.
Where it falls short, and what we would do next
1
A separate contract model
The current model flags 5 of 214 churners on one and two year contracts.
2
Calibrate the scores
Balanced weights inflate them. The medium tier scores 30 to 60%, and 26% of those customers churned.
3
A/B test the offers
Replace our 20% save rate assumption with a measured one.
4
Add behavior data
The two models tie, so new signal such as support calls beats a fancier algorithm.
